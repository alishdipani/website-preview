Neuro- & cognitive science for AI

Probing AI systems with controlled experiments from human cognition and psychophysics, and with tools from cognitive neuroscience.

AI for neuro- & cognitive science

Building AI models that predict human brain activity and behavior, and agents that use them to design experiments and test hypotheses about the brain.

Currently, I'm turning toward AI safety, starting with whether vision-language models know when they are wrong.

I'm a NeuroAI PhD student at Georgia Tech, advised by Apurva Ratan Murty and Dobromir Rahnev, and supported by the CoCo Fellowship from the Center of Excellence in Computational Cognition. My background is in computer science, vision science, and computational cognitive neuroscience. Previously, I was a research assistant at MIT Brain and Cognitive Sciences, Northeastern University, and TCS Innovation Labs, and a machine learning engineer at Baylor College of Medicine. Beyond research, I develop open-source teaching material for Neuromatch Academy's courses in computational neuroscience, deep learning, and NeuroAI, and was a Google Summer of Code contributor in 2019.
