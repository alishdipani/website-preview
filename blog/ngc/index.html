Why NGC?#

Before we knew that the universe is bigger than the Milky Way, astronomers spent centuries looking up at the fuzzy patches of light in the sky, not knowing what they were. One such astronomer, John Louis Emil Dreyer, spent decades compiling the New General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars (abbreviated NGC), a catalogue of thousands of deep-sky objects. This catalogue laid the foundations of extragalactic astronomy by providing a vocabulary for researchers to share their observations. Yet it was far from perfect: it had duplicate entries, wrong positions, and some objects that don’t exist. Towards the end of Dreyer’s life, Edwin Hubble showed that one of its entries, NGC 224 (now known as the Andromeda galaxy), was in fact a galaxy, revealing the universe extended far beyond the Milky Way. Dreyer may have lived just long enough to understand the true nature of what he had observed.

I think of my hazy, often imperfect posts as entries in my own NGC. I hope one day these ideas and observations may serve as the basis for someone to reveal some truth about our universe. Meanwhile, those astronomers’ persistence reminds me to keep looking up.
